## Ready-to-record script

Hello, my name is Dhanesh Todarwal. This is my feedback and learning video for the Unified Mentor Data Analyst internship project, Customer Segmentation and Churn Pattern Analytics in European Banking.

The objective of this project was to understand which customer segments are more likely to churn and to turn those findings into a practical Streamlit dashboard. I worked with 10,000 European banking customer records covering geography, age, tenure, credit score, account balance, product count, activity status, salary, and the churn target.

I began by validating the dataset, checking missing values, duplicate customer IDs, category values, and binary fields. I then removed surname from the analytical outputs and created reusable segments for geography, age, credit score, tenure, balance, activity, and high-value customers.

The most important findings were that the overall churn rate was about 20.4 percent, Germany had the highest country-level churn rate at about 32.4 percent, and the 46 to 59 age group had a churn rate of about 51.1 percent. Inactive customers also showed a much higher churn rate than active customers. The strongest interaction was Germany customers aged 46 to 59, which should be investigated further before launching a retention campaign.

In the dashboard, I created an overall churn summary, geography and age visualizations, tenure and credit comparisons, high-value customer analysis, dynamic filters, and KPI updates. The high-value analysis is deliberately described as balance exposure rather than revenue loss because the dataset does not contain revenue or profit information.

My main learning from this project was how to move from a business problem to validated data, documented definitions, reproducible analysis, and an interactive decision-support dashboard. I also learned that counts and rates must be considered together, especially for small groups such as customers with three or four products.

There are some important limitations. The data is a 2025 snapshot, so it cannot show changes over time. The analysis shows associations and prioritization signals, not causal explanations. Geographic and demographic differences should support human investigation, not automatic adverse decisions.

Overall, this project helped me strengthen my skills in Python, pandas, data validation, segmentation, KPI design, visualization, Streamlit, and communicating insights to decision-makers. Thank you for reviewing my project.
